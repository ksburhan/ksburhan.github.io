# Lockpick: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

Lockpick is a puzzle game made by **Burhan Köseler**, based in **Osaka**,
Japan ("I", "me"). This page explains what data the game handles, why, and
what you can do about it. Questions: **lockpick@ksburhan.com**.

I handle personal information in line with Japan's Act on the Protection of
Personal Information (APPI). For players in the EU, UK and Switzerland, the
GDPR and its UK and Swiss equivalents also apply.

## The short version

- Your progress, boosters and settings stay on your device.
- Play analytics are **off unless you say yes**. They are anonymous, and you
  can switch them off at any time in the game.
- From level 30 the game shows ads: a banner at the bottom of each level, and
  short videos **only when you tap "Watch ad"** for a booster. The
  ads come from Google AdMob, which collects data as described below. In
  the EU, UK and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent first. On iPhone,
  Apple asks whether apps may track you.
- There are no accounts. I never ask for your name or email, and I don't
  sell data.

## 1. Data stored on your device

The game saves your progress, best results, your reward meter, boosters,
which tutorials you've seen, your settings, and which boosters you got from
ads on your current level (one of each per level). This data stays on your
device. It is only copied off the
device by your phone's own backups, if you have them turned on. Deleting the
game deletes it.

## 2. Play analytics (optional)

When you first open the game, it asks whether you want to share anonymous
play data. You can change your answer any time with **"Share play data"** in
**Options** on the home screen.

**What is sent if you say yes:**
- **Game events:** levels started, solved or left, moves, the pick straining
  or snapping, the reward meter and the boosters it gave, boosters used,
  tutorials shown, and ad offers and whether an ad was watched.
- **App and device details:** the app version, the device model, the
  operating system version, your language and region setting, and the color
  theme you picked.
- **IDs:** a random install ID and a session ID, both created by the game.

**What is not sent:** your name, email, contacts, precise location, or your
device's advertising ID. The analytics service discards IP addresses and
does not store them.

**Who receives it:** PostHog, which hosts this data in its EU cloud
([PostHog privacy policy](https://posthog.com/privacy)).

**Why:** to see where the game is too hard or too easy, and to fix it.

**Legal basis:** your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any
time by switching "Share play data" off. Switching it off also deletes any
events that haven't been sent yet.

**How long it's kept:** up to 12 months, the limit of the PostHog plan the
game uses.

## 3. Ads (Google AdMob)

From level 30, a banner ad shows at the bottom of each level, and the game
can offer a short video ad in exchange for a booster; videos only
play after you tap "Watch ad". The ads are provided by
Google AdMob: Google Ireland Limited for users in the EEA, UK and Switzerland,
and Google LLC elsewhere.

**What Google may collect when an ad is requested or shown:** device and
advertising identifiers (on iPhone, the advertising ID only if you allow
tracking), your IP address and the approximate location derived from it,
device and app information, and how you interact with the ad. Google uses
this to show and measure ads, to prevent fraud and, if you agree, to
personalize ads. The game itself never receives these identifiers. Details:

- [How Google uses information from apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites)
- [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)

**Your choices:**
- **EEA, UK and Switzerland:** before the first ad, Google's consent form
  asks how your data may be used for ads. You can change your choice at any
  time with **"Privacy choices"** in **Options** on the game's home screen.
- **iPhone:** Apple's "Allow tracking?" prompt decides whether the
  advertising ID can be used. You can change this later in iOS
  **Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking**.
- If you say no, you can still watch ads. They are just less personalized.

**Legal basis:** your consent where the law requires it, which Google's
consent form collects (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

## 4. Where your data is processed

- **Play analytics** are stored on PostHog's EU servers.
- **Ad data** is processed by Google in the United States and other
  countries where Google operates. Google's privacy policy explains how it
  protects data it moves between countries.

These countries' data protection rules can differ from Japan's and from
yours.

## 5. Children

Lockpick is a general-audience game. It is not directed at children under
13, and I don't knowingly collect data from them.

## 6. Your rights

You can ask me to disclose, correct, stop using or delete your personal
information.
- **In Japan:** the Personal Information Protection Commission handles
  complaints.
- **In the EU or UK:** you also have the right to:
  - restrict or object to the use of your data;
  - receive your data in a portable form;
  - withdraw your consent;
  - complain to your local data protection authority.

The analytics data is linked only to a random ID that the game doesn't show,
so I usually can't find a particular person's data. Switching "Share play
data" off stops collection immediately. If you need help, write to me at the
email address above. For ad data, use Google's tools linked in section 3.

## 7. Changes

If the game starts handling data differently, I'll update this page and the
date at the top.
